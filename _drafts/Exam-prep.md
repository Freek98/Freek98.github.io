# A guide to writing an exam

- Friendly introduction, knowledge should be shared. Maybe anecdote about eating fish and sleeping with a book under your pillow. 

A good friend once told me that when she was teaching a class and got a good question, she went up to the board and shared the question and her answer with the group. The idea is that knowledge should be shared. Although I sometimes forget to apply this when I TA, I recently got a very good question in a class, and I'd like to share the answer here. 
The class was an intro to Python course, one of the first university-level courses the students ever followed. The question was whether I have any tips for preparing for the exam. 

### Be well-rested 

- Go to bed on time, be nice to yourself. Have some nice food (no alcohol) on the evening before your exam and go to bed on time. Did I emphasize the importance of enough sleep? 

- Prepare early. I was surprised to get this question from some students 2 weeks before the exam. Maybe throw in the anecdote about your neighbour who got a call in the evening before an exam, and told the student to read chapters 13,14,15 (which happened to be all the material for the exam). Do not try to take shortcuts, there are none. However.... 

- Know what will be on the exam, look at old exams if they are available, but do this at the end of studying (you want to walk away from the course with more than just passing the exam, even though that's maybe not how it feels when the exam is a looming priority). A good course has the exam requirements easily findable on it's course website. 

- Celebrate afterwards. If you have friends in the course and think you have the social energy, plan something easy together (but let it be okay if they decide to leave early if they're tired). Sitting an exam/ Surviving an exam week is a long and mentally exhausting activity. Give yourself a break afterwards. You don't have to overdo it or put pressure on yourself to have fun though.
